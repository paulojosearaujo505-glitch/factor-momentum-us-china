Data and Methodology

This study utilizes monthly factor return data from two distinct markets. For the United States, data was collected from the Kenneth R. French Data Library, spanning from July 1963 to December 2025. For the Chinese A-share market, data was collected from the CSMAR (China Stock Market & Accounting Research) database. Due to data availability constraints, the Chinese sample covers a consecutive five-year period from January 2021 to December 2025.
To ensure an unbiased comparison, the U.S. sample is evaluated under two distinct horizons. First, the strategy is tested over the full historical U.S. sample to establish a long-term baseline of factor momentum performance. Second, to account for the limited Chinese data, the U.S. strategy is evaluated over a matched five-year sub-sample (2021–2025). This parallel construction allows for a direct, apples-to-apples comparison of factor momentum dynamics between the two markets during the most recent market regime.

The strategy is constructed using the five standard Fama-French factors, which represent distinct systematic risk premia:
1.	Market Excess Return (Mkt-RF): The return of the market portfolio minus the one-month Treasury bill rate.
2.	Size (SMB): Small Minus Big, capturing the return spread between small-cap and large-cap firms.
3.	Value (HML): High Minus Low, capturing the return spread between value (high book-to-market) and growth (low book-to-market) firms.
4.	Profitability (RMW): Robust Minus Weak, capturing the spread between firms with strong and weak operating profitability.
5.	Investment (CMA): Conservative Minus Aggressive, capturing the spread between firms that invest conservatively versus aggressively.

For the Chinese market, the CSMAR factor construction is designed to account for the structural realities of the A-share market. As documented by Liu, Stambaugh, and Yuan (2019), mechanically applying U.S. Fama-French definitions to Chinese data yields biased results due to the prevalence of "shell" companies among the smallest firms. The CSMAR factor models utilized in this study account for these structural differences, using free-float (negotiable shares) weighting to provide a clean measure of the underlying risk premia.

The Factor Momentum (FMOM) strategy is a time-series momentum strategy applied at the factor level. The methodology follows the standard momentum construction (Jegadeesh & Titman, 1993; Ehsani & Linnainmaa, 2022). At the end of each month t, the trailing 12-month cumulative return is calculated for each of the five factors.
The portfolio is constructed with a one-month holding period and is rebalanced monthly. The allocation rule is as follows:
•	The strategy takes a long position in any factor that generated a positive cumulative return over the trailing 12 months.
•	The strategy takes a short position in any factor that generated a negative cumulative return over the trailing 12 months.
•	If a factor’s trailing return is exactly zero, it is assigned a weight of zero.
To ensure the strategy is dollar-neutral and not simply a proxy for market beta, the long and short legs are equal-weighted independently. For example, if three factors have positive trailing returns and two have negative returns, the long positions receive a total weight of +1.0 (each weighted at +0.333) and the short positions receive a total weight of -1.0 (each weighted at -0.500).

The performance of the FMOM strategy is evaluated using standard financial metrics. The primary metric is the annualized Sharpe ratio, calculated as the ratio of the annualized mean return to the annualized standard deviation of returns. Additionally, the analysis reports the annualized mean return, annualized volatility, and maximum drawdown to capture the risk-adjusted performance and tail-risk profile of the strategy. All calculations are performed on monthly returns and annualized using standard scaling factors (12 for returns and 12 for volatility)
