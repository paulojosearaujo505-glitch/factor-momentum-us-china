Introduction 
This paper investigates whether factor premia exhibit time-series momentum in U.S. and China equity markets. Specifically, it aims to determine if a trading strategy that goes long the five standard Fama-French factors (Market Risk Premium, Size, Value, Profitability, and Investment) with positive trailing 12-month returns and shorts those with negative returns generates significant risk-adjusted returns. The primary research question is: Do the Fama-French factors exhibit momentum, and does the efficacy of this factor momentum strategy differ significantly between the U.S. and Chinese A-share markets?

My interest in this research unfolded in class shortly after learning the Fama-French three-factor model (SMB, HML, and the market risk premium). Weeks later, while reviewing additional asset pricing paper, I noticed how widespread these factors were across empirical finance. To solidify my core understanding, I revisited and reconstructed the empirical framework of Fama and French (1993).  

Later, while collaborating with my classmate for the group presentation about international momentum and culture—focusing on Jegadeesh and Titman (1993)—I began exploring how momentum integrates into multi-factor settings. While Jegadeesh and Titman (1993) established individual stock momentum and Carhart (1997) formalized it into a four-factor model, recent literature has shifted this analytical lens. Notably, Ehsani and Linnainmaa (2022) demonstrate that stock momentum is largely driven by momentum in the underlying factors themselves. However, a significant gap remains in the literature: factor momentum has primarily been documented separately in the U.S. and China, but rarely with a unified, like-for-like specification.  

China serves as an ideal testing ground for this theory. Standard stock-level momentum is famously weak or non-existent in the Chinese A-share market, a phenomenon often attributed to collectivist cultural dynamics (Chui, Titman, & Wei, 2010). By applying an identical factor momentum strategy to both markets, this study provides a crucial test: if factor momentum succeeds in China where stock momentum fails, it provides strong evidence that factor-level trends capture a deeper, more universal economic reality.  

This study applies a quantitative and empirical research design. The methodology involves downloading monthly factor return data from the Kenneth R. French Data Library for the U.S. and the CSMAR database for China. The trading strategy is a replication that borrows from three foundational papers discussed in class: the factors are defined by Fama and French (1993), the 12-month momentum signal is adapted from Jegadeesh and Titman (1993), and the application of momentum to the factors themselves follows Ehsani and Linnainmaa (2022). This methodology results in a dollar-neutral, equal-weighted portfolio that rebalances monthly. Performance is measured using annualized returns, volatility, Sharpe ratios, and maximum drawdowns, with robustness checks applied for transaction costs and alternative lookback periods. Due to data availability constraints, the Chinese sample covers 2021–2025; to ensure an apples-to-apples comparison, the U.S. strategy is evaluated both over its full historical horizon (1963–present) and a matched five-year window
